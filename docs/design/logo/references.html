John, after round 2: "doesn't really give off the feeling of being a text completion tool. Can you give me some references for icons of well designed saas software and such?" Icons as their makers ship them, from the App Store or their own sites.

TL;DR: icons that say "text" use a caret, a letter, or a writing object. The knots say "brand".
  1. Round 2 lost the caret, and that's why it stopped feeling like typing. The woven loops sit in the same family as ChatGPT's knot. Every tool here that reads as "writing" keeps a caret (iA Writer, Cotypist), a letter (Grammarly, TextExpander, Notion), or a page (Drafts, Fantastical).
  2. A simple mark can work: iA Writer is just a caret. What carries it is the finish. A white glossy tile reads as a page, and the caret has light and depth. Our A was flat on a dark tile, which reads as a terminal.
  3. The craft is in the material. Things, Superhuman, Drafts and Cursor all render one object with light, depth and a soft shadow, in the macOS 26 style. None of our drawn options did that.
  4. Cotypist, the direct competitor, uses a mascot holding a caret. Being "the one without a robot" is a clear way to stand apart.

For round 3, I suggest: (a) iA Writer's lesson, our caret and ghost text on a glossy paper tile in G's colours, rendered with depth; (b) a Tab keycap with grey suggestion text on it, because Tab is how you take a suggestion and no other icon here uses it; (c) a letter: 잇 or "it" with the last stroke as ghost text.

Cotypist

The direct competitor: inline completions on the Mac. A mascot holding a text cursor, so it says "typing helper" at once, though the robot is busy at 16 px.

iA Writer: Get Focused.

One cyan caret on a white tile. As simple as our A, and it works: the caret is the whole message, and the glossy white tile makes it feel like a page.

A letter G inside a speech-bubble shape. Says "writing" by using a letter.

A lowercase t with a little flag: a letter in motion, so it reads as text that expands.
