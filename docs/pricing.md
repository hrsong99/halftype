## Pro, later: only if the fake door shows demand
- **What it is:** Personal Model, a LoRA trained on the user's own text (sent Mail, Messages, a KakaoTalk export, then what they type) and retrained as it grows.
- **Trained on a rented GPU, not the Mac (changed 2026-11-09):** on-device training depends on a laptop we can't see, and when it's idle; a bad experience. The Mac sends the training rows over TLS to a serverless GPU (Modal), which keeps them only in the job's `/tmp`, deletes them before it returns, logs counts only, and sends back the adapter, which stays on the Mac. It works for John's own adapter now: `lab/cloud/` in the private `hrsong99/halftype-train` repo. **Cost per run, measured 2026-11-09:** John's 3,014 messages (493k tokens), Kanana on an L4, trained in 448 s, ~10 min billed with start-up and model load: ~$0.15 at $0.80/h, inside Modal's $30/month free credit. Validation loss 2.63, against the Mac's 2.61. **Before anyone else's text goes up (checked 2026-11-10):** Modal keeps every Function's inputs and outputs for up to 7 days, encrypted at rest ([security](https://modal.com/docs/guide/security)), so today's `train.py`, which passes the rows as a call argument and returns the adapter, leaves both there for a week: the README's "deleted when it ends, with no copy kept" is not met yet. Modal's Servers and Endpoints store no payloads, and passing a reference instead of the data keeps it out ([data residency](https://modal.com/docs/guide/data-residency)): send the rows and fetch the adapter through one of those, and confirm with Modal in writing. Modal's [DPA](https://modal.com/legal/dpa) applies automatically with its terms (processing only to provide the service, staff access need-to-know, SCCs, subprocessors at trust.modal.com); its logs keep 1 day on Starter, and ours hold counts only. Still to write: our own privacy policy (PIPA, GDPR), naming Modal as a processor. The README and site already describe it as opt-in (2026-11-09); the app's own lines ("Nothing you type is sent", Window.swift) change in the release that adds the upload. Confidential GPUs (attested, as Apple's Private Cloud Compute) are the stronger promise, at ~5-9× the price; Colab and Kaggle are not for customer text.
- **Price (a guess, not tested):** $4/month, $36–40/year, $99 lifetime. Lifetime is set at ~2.5× the yearly price, as Superwhisper does (~2.9×). At 2× (for example $80), most buyers would take lifetime and the recurring revenue would never come.
- **Store: a merchant of record, not GitHub Sponsors (decided 2026-11-09).** Sponsors takes no fee, but buyers need a GitHub account (most Halftype users aren't developers), it has no yearly plan, a licence key makes a sponsorship a sale whose Korean 부가세 and EU VAT would be John's to file, and GitHub's terms don't say licence-key rewards are allowed. Check Polar first (built for open source, licence keys, yearly plans, ~4% + 40¢; Korea payouts unconfirmed), else Lemon Squeezy (5% + 50¢, pays out to Korea). Selling Pro is a business: register (홈택스, free) before launch. Sponsors stay donations.
- **The licence check works offline** (a signed key checked locally). Training is new network use, so it's opt-in and named in the README.
- **Retraining cadence:** when someone's history has grown by about half, not every week. The gain was ~+1.1 points per tripling of history (`lab/PLAYBOOK.md` in halftype-train), so weekly retrains add almost nothing.
- **Fake door first:** a "Personal Model: $4/mo" row in the window, that opens the early-bird form in the browser: the app can't count clicks without new network use, so form sign-ups against running installs (`scripts/usage.sh`) are the measure, before building the trainer or billing.
